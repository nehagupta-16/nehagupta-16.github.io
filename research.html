Most of what I do starts with the same problem: a bulk experiment averages away exactly the cells you care about. Single-cell and single-nucleus sequencing gives that resolution back, but it also hands you a dataset that is noisy, sparse and easy to over-interpret. A good deal of my work is simply the discipline of getting from those reads to a claim you'd be willing to defend.

The other half is structural. Once you know a kinase is behaving strangely in a particular cell type, the natural next question is why — and that's a question about the protein itself. Being able to move between the transcript and the structure is the part of this work I find most satisfying.

What I work on

Single-cell & isoform transcriptomics scRNA-seq · snRNA-seq · bulk RNA-seq

Resolving kinase expression and splicing at the level of individual neuronal cell types — clustering and integration in Seurat, batch correction with Harmony, differential expression with DESeq2, and isoform-level quantification where the transcript matters as much as the gene.

Kinase structure & function AlphaFold3 · PyMOL

Characterising functional motifs, catalytic machinery and autoregulation in kinases that haven't had much attention. I contributed to work on DCLK3 — an understudied kinase linked to neurodegeneration — deciphering how it regulates itself and what it prefers as a substrate.

Drug discovery & docking Molecular docking · MD · protein modeling

My earliest research was in silico drug discovery, and it still shapes how I think about a target — docking and dynamics as a way of asking whether an idea is even chemically plausible before anyone spends a year on it. My first paper came out of this: steroidal alkaloids from Holarrhena pubescens modelled against guanylyl cyclase C.

Neurodegeneration & neuroimmunology The through-line

What actually goes wrong in the ageing and diseased brain, and which molecular players we can act on. At the Broad I worked on TIM-3, an immune checkpoint that turns out to regulate microglia in Alzheimer's disease; at UGA the focus is kinase dysfunction in neuronal cells.
