Namaste, I'm Neha.

I'm a PhD candidate in bioinformatics at the University of Georgia, which mostly means I spend my days trying to get very large, very messy genomic datasets to tell me something honest about the brain. Before Athens I was in Boston, and before that, India.

This site is partly a record of that work. It's also here because I don't think a CV says very much about a person, and I'd rather you got the fuller version.

How I got here

I studied biotechnology at Amity University in Noida, where I got interested in bioinformatics and the idea of using computers to study biology. I worked on molecular docking and RNA-sequencing projects.

In 2020 I moved to Boston for a master's at Boston University — new country, new city, and a pandemic, all at once. I interned at the Broad Institute, analysing single-cell and single-nucleus data from Alzheimer's mouse models; that work helped define a microglial subpopulation and became part of a 2025 Nature paper on the immune checkpoint TIM-3. I then spent a year and a half at Cellarity, a drug-discovery company in Somerville, where I learned what it means to build pipelines and QC that other people depend on.

In 2023 I came to the University of Georgia for my PhD, in Natarajan Kannan's lab. I work on kinases: how they're expressed and spliced in individual neuronal cell types, how their networks shift in Alzheimer's disease, and what their structures tell us about why. I also build pipelines for UGA's Computational Biology Consulting Group, and I'm part of a team developing a multi-agent LLM system that generates testable hypotheses about kinase–substrate specificity in cancer.

The thing that keeps me in this field is that neurodegeneration is still genuinely unsolved. We're only now able to look at it one cell at a time, and that feels like being handed a much better microscope right when it matters.

Alongside the research, I served as President of UGA's Indian Student Association for 2024–25 and was named Student Leader of the Year by International Student Life. I've mentored an NSF REU student through a summer project, and I sit on the Institute of Bioinformatics' faculty speaker nomination committee.
