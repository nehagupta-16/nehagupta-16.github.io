Hello — I'm Neha.

I'm a PhD candidate in bioinformatics at the University of Georgia, which mostly means I spend my days trying to get very large, very messy genomic datasets to tell me something honest about the brain. Before Athens I was in Boston, and before that, India.

This site is partly a record of that work. It's also here because I don't think a CV says very much about a person, and I'd rather you got the fuller version.

How I got here

I studied biotechnology at Amity University in Noida, expecting to spend my life at a bench. What actually happened is that I found the computational side of biology somewhere in the middle of my degree and never really left it. My first proper job was as a bioinformatics analyst in Indore, building RNA-seq pipelines for colon cancer samples — the first time I understood that a well-built analysis can be its own kind of craft.

In 2020 I moved to Boston for a master's at Boston University, which turned out to be a much bigger move than I'd planned for — new country, new city, and a pandemic. I interned at the Broad Institute working on microglia and Alzheimer's disease, then spent a year and a half at Cellarity, a biotech in Somerville, where I learned what it means to build analysis infrastructure other people depend on.

In 2023 I came to the University of Georgia for my PhD, in Natarajan Kannan's lab. I work on kinases — specifically, how they behave in neuronal cells, and what goes wrong with them in neurodegenerative disease. It sits right where I like to be: half single-cell genomics, half protein structure.

The thing that keeps me in this field is that neurodegeneration is still genuinely unsolved. We're only now able to look at it one cell at a time, and that feels like being handed a much better microscope right when it matters.

Somewhere alongside all of that I ended up as President of UGA's Indian Student Association, which was less a line on a CV than a year of learning how to feed 400 people at a Diwali event and keep everyone speaking to each other. I was named Student Leader of the Year in 2025, and I'm still not entirely sure who nominated me.

Away from the screen

I play badminton, and I'm more competitive about it than anyone who has only met me at a whiteboard would guess. I bake — it's chemistry with a better output, and it's the one part of my life where following the protocol exactly is genuinely relaxing rather than obligatory. I'm teaching myself guitar, badly, which I recommend to anyone several years into a PhD: it is good to be a beginner at something again.

Three cities in ten years — Noida, Boston, Athens — has made me fairly good at arriving somewhere new and building a life from nothing. I think that's the part of me that shows up most in how I work.
